According to SecurityWeek, Ahold Delhaize estimates that the October cyberattack will reduce its fourth-quarter net sales by €200 million to €250 million and cost €75 million to €100 million in fourth-quarter underlying and IFRS (International Financial Reporting Standards) operating income.

## What happened

According to SecurityWeek, the company confirmed on October 19 that it had been hit by a cyberattack and said it had no evidence at the time that customer, associate or partner data had been stolen. It took some systems offline to protect its IT environment, mainly affecting online capabilities in the Benelux and the U.S. as well as Benelux supply-chain operations. The report says most of the disruption was contained within two weeks while work continued to fully restore normal operations.

## Why it matters beyond the quarter

According to SecurityWeek, the incident also reshaped the full-year outlook: Ahold Delhaize now projects 2026 diluted underlying earnings per share growth in the low single digits, rather than the mid to high single digits it had previously forecast. Free cash flow is now expected at around €2.1 billion, down from at least €2.3 billion, a reduction the company attributes to the cyberattack and to newly introduced Serbian rules governing payments to suppliers.

## What is still unknown

Ahold Delhaize has not disclosed what type of attack it experienced, according to SecurityWeek, so the report offers no indicators of compromise for other organizations to check. The report notes that in November 2024 a ransomware attack disrupted several of its U.S. brands, including Hannaford supermarkets and Giant Food pharmacies, and that the INC Ransom group claimed responsibility. Ahold Delhaize later disclosed that personal information of more than 2.2 million people had been compromised in that incident.
