TSMC says some current and former employee data was made available on an external website without authorization. The company said it found no evidence that its systems have been compromised. TSMC filed a statement with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) regarding the unauthorized access of employee personal data on November 12, 2026.

According to BleepingComputer, a threat actor put 6.9GB of information allegedly stolen from TSMC up for auction. The report said that on November 8, someone using the alias "SmithBianl" leaked a sample text file of 499 rows allegedly stolen from TSMC, and the actor claimed the file was a sample of a 6.9GB dataset containing 46,000 employee records. The report said bidding started at 3 BTC (bitcoin), approximately $65,000, and the actor set a first deadline of November 15 to leak the data for free if no buyer emerged.

## Why it matters

TSMC says the information matched data stored in the system services of a cloud service provider and is unrelated to its operations or customer-related data. The report said the leaked information allegedly included employees' national ID numbers, addresses, contact details, and banking information.

## Response so far

TSMC is informing impacted personnel and providing them with necessary support. The company has engaged the cloud service vendor, the relevant investigating authorities, and a third-party cybersecurity agency to ascertain the facts and take necessary actions.
