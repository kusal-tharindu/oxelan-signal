According to SecurityWeek, the Colorado Governor's Office of Information Technology (OIT) has disclosed a data breach impacting data that residents uploaded to the myColorado application. The report describes myColorado as Colorado's official mobile application, used for digital identification, state services, and document storage. The app has approximately 2.1 million users.

The app began experiencing unusual usage patterns late Wednesday night, Nov. 18, 2026. Automated threat detection systems within the Amazon Web Services (AWS) environment were triggered and alerted the OIT security team. OIT then discovered what appeared to be unauthorized activity and took the application down as a precautionary measure.

As of Nov. 25, 2026, OIT said the incident primarily involved documents within the “myCards and Documents” part of the application. Payment information was not involved. OIT estimates that roughly 100,000 users' data was involved.

## Why it matters

The accessed data is user-uploaded data, including PDFs and photographs of documents like passports, vehicle registrations, and insurance cards. OIT has said it has no reason to believe the incident is part of a bigger attack.

## What to do

Direct notifications to affected individuals are anticipated to begin on Friday, Nov. 27, 2026. The application remains offline, and OIT says it does not currently have a confirmed restoration timeline. OIT says it will continue to provide updates as they become available.
