## What changed

According to BleepingComputer, a threat actor using the "ShinyHunters" handle listed records allegedly belonging to 335 million Binance users for sale. The seller claimed a dataset of 335,900,000 lines associated with users in approximately 170 countries and asked $900,000 in Bitcoin or Monero, with a threat to publish the material unless Binance paid.

## What Binance says

Binance's preliminary review characterized the listing as a likely scam and found no indication that the advertised information demonstrated a breach of its systems. Binance founder Changpeng Zhao dismissed the claim as "obviously fake/scam". Binance said it found no evidence that its systems, customer Know Your Customer (KYC) data, account credentials, wallet private keys, or internal infrastructure were compromised in connection with the listing.

According to the report, the allegedly stolen information includes KYC documents, internal infrastructure information, account passwords, crypto wallet private keys, transaction history, emails, and phone numbers. The exchange said it does not hold private keys for users' external wallets and does not store user passwords in plaintext.

## Why it matters

Binance has also described a separate incident in its November 3 incident report: a $6.34 billion theft on October 29 involving unauthorized access to its wallet signing service. The FBI attributed the Binance wallet breach to the TraderTraitor North Korean state-backed hacking group, also tracked as Lazarus Group, Jade Sleet and APT38. TraderTraitor was previously linked to the theft of $1.5 billion in the February 2025 Bybit hack.

## What to do

Binance advises customers never to share passwords, two-factor authentication (2FA) codes, seed phrases, or private keys, and to stay wary of phishing attempts. According to the report, similar past listings have often involved infostealer malware data collected from users' own devices rather than a breach of Binance systems.
