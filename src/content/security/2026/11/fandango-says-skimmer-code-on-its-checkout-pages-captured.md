Fandango Media, LLC reported a data security incident to the California Attorney General on November 13, 2026. The filing lists a breach date of October 8, 2026. According to BleepingComputer, the ticket seller is notifying customers about a data breach that exposed payment card details entered on its checkout pages.

## What is affected

The report says the exposed details were entered between October 8 and 11, 2026, and that the incident affects customers who made purchases through Fandango's official website. Card skimmer code injected into the checkout page intercepted payment card details entered when placing orders and sent the data to hackers. In the notification quoted by the outlet, Fandango said its investigation identified evidence that an unauthorized party could access payment card information associated with certain transactions on its checkout page.

## What may have been exposed

The data types that may have been exposed, per the report, include payment card number, CVV (card verification value), expiration date, billing ZIP code and email address. Names, government-issued identity information, Social Security Numbers and other account information were not exposed. Fandango says it determined on October 30, 2026 that customer information may have been impacted.

## Why it matters

Fandango sells tickets through its website and app for 31,000 U.S. cinema screens and also operates the movie review site Rotten Tomatoes, according to the report. Fandango says it has fixed the issue on its checkout page and investigated with outside cybersecurity specialists, and that it is currently unaware of any cases of fraud leveraging the stolen data.

## What to do

Customers who made purchases through Fandango's official website can check whether their order falls inside the window the company described. According to the report, Fandango is offering two years of free IDX credit and dark web monitoring, as well as identity theft recovery services. The enrollment deadline is February 13, 2027. Fandango has not shared the number of affected customers.
