According to SecurityWeek, Japan announced an emergency cybersecurity package on Friday that combines artificial intelligence (AI) with dedicated response teams. The report puts the package at 10 billion yen ($63 million) and says its measures are designed to build a resilient society.

Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi told a ministerial meeting on Friday that a rapid response to potential attacks is essential in the era of AI-generated attacks. She said unauthorized access and data breaches are occurring at this very moment, causing a major impact on daily lives and corporate activities. Japan has faced a barrage of high-profile cyberattacks this year, attributed to the rise of AI and to security system vulnerabilities exacerbated by poor cyber hygiene.

## What is in the package

A "top gun" team of cybersecurity experts will be set up by the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry by the end of November. Team members will use AI to counter hackers and detect weaknesses in network systems. The government will also enhance its capability to access servers used by hackers as a preemptive measure known as "active cyber defense," which was legalized in October.

## Why it matters

The National Cybersecurity Office (NCO) was established in July 2025, and the Cybersecurity Capability Enhancement Act and related legislation were enacted in May 2025. The Cybersecurity Strategic Headquarters established the Common Cybersecurity Standards for Critical Infrastructure on July 31, 2026, and those standards entered into force on October 1, 2026.

## What to do

Companies affected by cyberattacks will get initial responses from a "cyber rescue team" of experts from the public and private sectors. Where there is a possible challenge to Japan's national security, the government will deploy a support team of officials from the Defense Ministry, the National Police Agency and the National Cybersecurity Office.
