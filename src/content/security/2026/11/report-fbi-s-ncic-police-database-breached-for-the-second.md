According to BleepingComputer, FBI systems have reportedly been breached for the second time in two months, this time targeting the agency's National Crime Information Center (NCIC). The NCIC is an FBI-operated database that law enforcement agencies across the United States use to share criminal justice records.

The intrusion was reportedly discovered in recent days. The report says it is unclear when the breach occurred or how the attackers gained access. It also remains unclear whether the attackers accessed sensitive law enforcement records stored in NCIC, or whether the intrusion affected the system's availability.

The FBI has not publicly confirmed the incident. According to the report, the FBI is investigating an apparent breach of the NCIC system, citing two people briefed on the investigation.

## Why it matters

The NCIC is used by law enforcement agencies across the United States to share criminal justice records, so the reported intrusion involves a system shared well beyond a single agency.

Two months earlier, the ShinyHunters extortion group claimed to have breached FBI systems by exploiting an unpatched Oracle PeopleSoft vulnerability, the report says. That earlier attack targeted a third-party platform associated with the FBI's recruitment website, FBIJobs.gov. The FBI investigation into that breach led to arrests of suspected ShinyHunters members.

There is currently no indication that the NCIC incident is connected to ShinyHunters or the earlier breach, according to the report.

## What to do

The cited reporting contains no patch, workaround, or mitigation step; the FBI is investigating an apparent breach of the NCIC system. The FBI has not publicly confirmed the incident and did not respond to the request for comment, per the report. Whether records were accessed or availability was affected is not yet known.
