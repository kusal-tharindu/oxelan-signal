## What happened

OpenAI personnel discovered a surreptitious audio recording device inserted into a USB port on the back of a television in an executive conference room on or about September 30, 2026. The device had been concealed in toilet paper, was installed on or about September 28, 2026, and its contents were encrypted. Abdulrahman Alfozan, 35, a national of the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia, was arrested on October 9, 2026. He had been employed as an engineer by OpenAI since July 2025.

Footage from closed-circuit television (CCTV) inside the OpenAI office showed Alfozan near the executive conference room on multiple dates prior to the device's discovery, though he had no authority to access it. On October 5, 2026, he entered the room and appeared to look behind the television into which the device had been inserted. On or about the same day, a second, similar recording device was found in his unattended bag.

## The charge

Alfozan was charged with using or endeavoring to use an electronic, mechanical, or other device to intercept oral communications. According to The Hacker News, a grand jury indicted him on a single count of interception of oral communications, and he pleaded not guilty in San Francisco federal court.

According to the report, he has been held without bail since his arrest. The report also said he was arrested three days before he, his wife and son were scheduled to fly one-way from San Francisco to Riyadh, and that his wife and son left the United States without him, departing San Francisco about 36 hours after the arrest. The report also said court documents alleged he was in frequent contact with a member of the Saudi Royal Family via WhatsApp.

## What happens next

If convicted, Alfozan faces a maximum of 5 years in prison and a $250,000 fine, followed by 3 years of supervised release. According to the report, his next hearing is scheduled for November 17, 2026.
