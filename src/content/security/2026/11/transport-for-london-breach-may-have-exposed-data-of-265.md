## What happened

According to BleepingComputer, Transport for London (TfL) announced that around 265,000 Oyster photocard customers may have had their account details exposed in a data breach. TfL discovered the security incident on November 16, 2026, the report says.

## What is affected

According to the report, the exposed data may include full names, phone numbers, email addresses, home addresses, dates of birth, and security questions and answers. TfL's assessment is that passwords, bank and payment card details, journey information, and names and dates of birth of children with Zip Oyster cards were not compromised.

## Why it matters

TfL says it has found no evidence so far that the affected data has been misused. The report notes that in 2024, Scattered Spider members breached TfL's network and stole customer data and disrupted online services, including Oyster refunds. That attack cost TfL £29 million in losses and recovery costs.

## What to do

TfL says it has contained the incident and notified the UK's Information Commissioner's Office (ICO). The Oyster photocard website has been taken offline. Once the portal is restored, all photocard customers will be prompted to change their password, security question, and answer. Affected customers should make those changes when prompted.
