## What happened

The U.S. Department of Justice (DOJ) announced coordinated actions against the Democratic People's Republic of Korea's (DPRK) cryptocurrency theft schemes. They include a court-authorized seizure and restraint of more than $1.2 billion worth of cryptocurrency recently stolen from Binance, and the Federal Bureau of Investigation's (FBI) seizure of cloud-based infrastructure used in FaceStealer, a social engineering scheme that targets people rather than software flaws.

## The Binance theft

According to the FBI, on or about Oct. 29, members of the North Korean military, referred to by the FBI as TraderTraitor, stole more than $6 billion worth of virtual assets from the Binance cryptocurrency exchange. According to SecurityWeek, the theft of about $6.3 billion from Binance is regarded as the biggest crypto heist in history. The report also states that the FBI has previously attributed the theft to the North Korean hacking group known as TraderTraitor.

## FaceStealer

The FaceStealer scheme has resulted in the theft of more than $400 million worth of cryptocurrency. Pursuant to a federal warrant, the FBI seized over 900 webcam videos of FaceStealer victims.

## Why it matters

Collective U.S. government and private sector efforts so far have resulted in the freeze of more than $2.7 billion of the stolen funds. The hackers behind FaceStealer target individuals working in the cryptocurrency and Web3 industries, and increasingly those involved in open-source software development.

## What to do

The FBI, in coordination with the U.S. Secret Service (USSS) and Internal Revenue Service Criminal Investigation (IRS-CI), issued a Cyber Alert urging virtual asset service providers (VASPs) to block transactions tied to TraderTraitor laundering addresses. The FBI also created a website at www.fbi.gov/facestealer as a centralized hub for resources and information on the FaceStealer scheme.
