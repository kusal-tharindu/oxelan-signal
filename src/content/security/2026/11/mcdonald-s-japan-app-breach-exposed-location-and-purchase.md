According to BleepingComputer, McDonald's Japan disclosed a data breach after attackers accessed a database containing sensitive information for users of its official smartphone app. McDonald's Japan confirmed that third parties gained unauthorized access to part of the customer-related information in the official app's database. The report says the exposed information includes users' product purchase history, IP (Internet Protocol) addresses, mobile device location data, and customer management IDs (identifiers).

## What is affected

The exposed data does not include affected customers' names, email addresses, phone numbers, home addresses, or credit card information, according to the report. McDonald's Japan said a follow-up investigation found no service disruption or misuse of official apps as a result of the incident. The affected product is the official smartphone app, whose database held the customer-related information.

## Why it matters

The report places the disclosure alongside earlier breaches at McDonald's and Burger King Japan. The report says McDonald's disclosed a data breach in June 2021 after attackers breached its systems and stole information belonging to employees and customers from the US (United States), South Korea, and Taiwan. The report also says Burger King Japan disclosed a major data breach after attackers stole 11.18 million customer records that were later leaked online. Macnica researcher Yutaka Sejiyama has logged 288 cybersecurity incidents affecting Japanese organizations involving personal information theft or exposure since the start of the year, according to the report.

## What to do

McDonald's Japan said it is working to invalidate credentials used for unauthorized access, investigate the relevant systems, and report to the Personal Information Protection Commission. The company also stated it will strengthen security measures and prevent recurrence.
