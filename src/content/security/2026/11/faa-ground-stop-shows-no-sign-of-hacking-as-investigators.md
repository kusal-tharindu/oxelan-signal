According to SecurityWeek, the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) issued a nationwide ground stop on November 13 after a communications problem hit its air route traffic control centers. The order was lifted at 8:15 pm ET, more than four hours later, after disrupting thousands of flights. No official root cause has been announced.

## What officials have said

According to the report, US officials have found no evidence that hackers caused the outage. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said the incident did not appear to be the result of a cyberattack and that the failure appeared to be mechanical. Mullin said there was no chatter and no footprints indicating an attack, but that officials were still looking and would not rule one out.

The report adds that the FBI does not think a cyberattack or criminal activity was behind the outage, and that investigators are looking at a software update as a potential cause. CBS News, cited in the same report, said the outage followed an update to the private communications network that links the air traffic control system; that network rebooted and then crashed and is operated by L3Harris.

## Why it matters

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy blamed legacy equipment, saying switches and routers were flooded with data and began producing inconsistent information, at which point the agency shut the system down. Mullin said the FAA had been applying patches and installing new equipment, which was overwhelming the older system. Officials also said the incident had nothing to do with the Microsoft outage earlier in the week and that the FAA's recently deployed AI systems played no role.

The previous nationwide ground stop came in January 2023, after a contractor unintentionally deleted files while working on the system that delivers safety notices to pilots, and no evidence of a cyberattack was found in that case either.

## What to do

The National Security Agency (NSA), the Department of Defense, CISA (the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency) and other agencies have been involved in the investigation, according to the report. There is no fixed version, workaround or operator step for this outage in the cited sources.
