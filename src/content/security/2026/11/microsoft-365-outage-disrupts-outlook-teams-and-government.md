According to SecurityWeek, Microsoft's outage involving services such as Outlook and Teams dragged on for hours and affected users, companies and some government operations. The report said Microsoft's disruption affected a wide range of Microsoft 365 offerings that interact with its Exchange Online and email services. It also said users reported that Outlook, Teams and AI (artificial intelligence) assistant Copilot were down.

Microsoft said it had no indication that the outage was caused by malicious activity. Microsoft also said changes applied to improve serviceability were not taking as expected. Microsoft said in January that it had more than 450 million paid Microsoft 365 commercial subscriptions.

## Impact on public services

The British government said it was working closely with Microsoft to assess any impact on public services, with robust contingency plans in place. British police forces said they could not receive crime reports online and urged people to call emergency responders for urgent reports. The National Pharmacy Association said people could face delays getting prescriptions because the outage affected NHS (National Health Service) email systems.

In Australia, Home Affairs Minister Tony Burke said Microsoft had likened the disruption to the 2024 CrowdStrike outage, and that Microsoft did not believe it was dealing with a cyberattack. Singapore's Government Technology Agency said government emails were down, and the Philippines' Supreme Court said the outage affected some operations, including email responses. OpenAI and Anthropic each reported problems connecting with Microsoft 365 tools. Air India said flight operations were unaffected but it temporarily rerouted email services.

## What to do

Microsoft said it was seeing early stabilization and capacity improvements as recovery actions continued. Follow Microsoft's service health updates for the current status; the cited sources do not give a fixed version or a customer-side workaround.
