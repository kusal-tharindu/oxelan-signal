According to SecurityWeek, Texas dental services provider Access Dental Management (ADM) has disclosed a data breach impacting more than 340,000 individuals. The report says ADM provides dental care across Texas and in Illinois, Indiana, and New Mexico.

## What happened

The report states that ADM discovered the incident on May 5 and began an investigation with help from third-party forensic specialists. Between May 1 and May 4, hackers accessed the company's network and viewed or copied certain data, including personal information. ADM's review of the compromised information was completed on October 21.

The attacker accessed names, addresses, dates of birth, Social Security numbers, medical information, and health insurance information. ADM notified the Texas and Vermont Attorney General's Offices that 341,049 individuals were affected, 341,039 of them in Texas and 10 in Vermont. The WorldLeaks ransomware group claimed responsibility for the intrusion on June 5, according to the report.

## Why it matters

The exposed data types are the ones that make a breach expensive to unwind for the people in it: a birth date or a Social Security number does not change the way a password can. For a care provider, the record set also ties a person to treatment and insurance details, which the notification lists among the accessed data. SecurityWeek said it had emailed ADM's parent platform, Surge Private Equity, for additional information on the incident.

## What to do

ADM is sending written notification letters to the potentially affected individuals. Beyond those letters, the report describes no patch, configuration change, or other mitigation step for affected individuals to apply. Readers who work with a provider or vendor should treat a letter of this kind as the start of their own record of what was exposed and when.
