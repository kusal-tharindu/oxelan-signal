The U.S. Department of Justice (DOJ) announced coordinated actions against the cryptocurrency theft schemes of the Democratic People's Republic of Korea (DPRK). The announcement covers the FaceStealer social engineering scheme, which the department says has resulted in the theft of more than $400 million worth of cryptocurrency.

## Who is behind FaceStealer

The DOJ attributes the scheme to North Korean hackers known publicly as Sapphire Sleet, UNC1069, Dangerous Password, CryptoCore, Advanced Persistent Threat 38 (APT38), BlueNoroff, and Stardust Chollima. According to BleepingComputer, the FaceStealer campaign ran for 18 months before the U.S. seized the underlying cloud-based infrastructure.

## A separate Binance theft

Separately from the FaceStealer campaign, the DOJ said that on or about October 29, members of the North Korean military referred to by the Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) as TraderTraitor stole more than $6 billion worth of virtual assets from Binance, a cryptocurrency exchange. The department also announced the court-authorized seizure and restraint of more than $1.2 billion worth of cryptocurrency recently stolen from Binance. Collective U.S. government and private sector efforts have so far frozen more than $2.7 billion of the stolen assets.

## What the FBI published

Pursuant to a federal warrant, the FBI seized over 900 webcam videos of FaceStealer victims. The bureau also published a report detailing the scheme and created a resource page at www.fbi.gov/facestealer.

## What to do

The FBI report and the resource page at www.fbi.gov/facestealer are the place to start; the bureau describes the page as a centralized hub for resources and information on the scheme. BleepingComputer's report adds that the attacks used multiple social engineering techniques, including deepfakes (synthetic video or audio that impersonates a real person) and ClickFix, to target individuals in the cryptocurrency and Web3 sectors as well as open-source developers.
