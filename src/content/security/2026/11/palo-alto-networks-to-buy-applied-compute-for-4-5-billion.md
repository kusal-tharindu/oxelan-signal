According to SecurityWeek, Palo Alto Networks has agreed to acquire Applied Compute, an artificial intelligence (AI) infrastructure company, in an all-stock transaction valued at $4.5 billion. The report describes Applied Compute's platform as a way for companies to adapt existing open-weight AI models to their own data and run them in production.

## What the buyer plans

Palo Alto Networks plans to offer that platform to its security customers, with deployment options in public clouds and sovereign environments, according to the report. Applied Compute co-founder and CEO Yash Patil will become co-chief AI officer at Palo Alto Networks after the deal closes.

## Why it matters

The acquisition follows Palo Alto Networks' September purchase of Console, an AI-native platform for building agentic workflows and automating operational tasks using natural language, the report says. Palo Alto Networks also reported fiscal first-quarter revenue of $3.44 billion, up 39% year over year, and next-generation security annual recurring revenue of $9.73 billion, up 66%. It raised its full-year outlook and now expects fiscal 2027 revenue of $14.52 billion to $14.57 billion.

## What to do

The cited report describes an acquisition, not a vulnerability. Applied Compute will continue serving its existing customers, per the report. The transaction still requires regulatory clearance and other customary closing conditions, and the report expects it to close in Palo Alto Networks' fiscal third quarter of 2027.
