Coles Group Limited is investigating a cyber security incident, the company said in a statement current as at 16 November 2026, 8:30am. Based on its investigation to date, Coles says it has not identified evidence that customer, team or supplier data has been compromised or removed from its systems. The company says operations continue and customers can shop as normal in stores and online.

According to BleepingComputer, Coles is Australia's second-largest retailer. The report said the company is aware of Liquorland click-and-collect issues affecting a small number of customers.

## Why it matters

An incident at a retailer of that size puts attention on what the company can confirm publicly. According to BleepingComputer, Coles has not said when it discovered the incident or whether attackers have made a ransom demand, and no major threat group has claimed the attack. That leaves the discovery timeline and any ransom demand unresolved in the cited statements.

## What to do

Customers should treat messages requesting personal data with suspicion and use unique passwords, though Coles did not link that advice to the incident, according to BleepingComputer. The company's stated response is to continue working with the Australian Cyber Security Centre and global cyber security experts to contain and secure its IT environment. The cited statements do not name a patch or fixed version to apply.
