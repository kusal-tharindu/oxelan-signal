## What happened

The Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN), a bureau of the U.S. Treasury, issued an order cutting off MEXC Global's access to the U.S. financial system as part of Operation Economic Outcast. The order prohibits U.S. financial institutions from processing funds transfers involving the exchange. It was issued under section 9714(a) of the Combating Russian Money Laundering Act and takes effect 30 days after publication in the Federal Register.

According to BleepingComputer, Treasury cut off the MEXC Global crypto exchange from the U.S. financial system over sanctions evasion and links with cybercrime gangs. The report describes MEXC as a Seychelles-based exchange with more than 40 million users from more than 170 countries.

## Why it matters

Between May 2021 and May 2026, MEXC facilitated the movement of at least $16 billion in funds connected to sanctions evasion, cybercrime, and other illicit activity, according to the order. That total includes nearly $1.5 billion in transactions linked to Russian sanctions evasion, among them $232 million in direct transactions with U.S.-, EU-, and UK-sanctioned entities. MEXC was still processing transactions with the sanctioned Russian entity Garantex and its successor Grinex as recently as June 2026, more than four years after Garantex's April 2022 designation by the Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC).

The order also describes approximately $1 billion in direct transactions between MEXC and more than 40 identified Iranian convertible virtual currency (CVC) exchangers. It cites approximately $22 million in transactions for the Central Bank of Iran and the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps and its Qods Force.

## What to do

U.S. financial institutions are prohibited from processing funds transfers involving MEXC Global once the order takes effect 30 days after publication in the Federal Register. MEXC said it is aware of the order issued on November 6, 2026, and is reviewing it with its legal and compliance teams. The company said it has also begun an independent review of its anti-money laundering (AML) and sanctions controls.
