## What happened

According to SecurityWeek, Fandango is warning customers that payment card information from some purchases made through its checkout page between October 8 and 11, 2026 may have been accessed by an unauthorized party. The company's investigation identified evidence that an unauthorized party could access payment card information associated with certain transactions on that page during the same window.

Potentially exposed data includes the card number, CVV code, expiration date, billing ZIP code, and the customer's email address. According to the report, customer names, Social Security numbers, government-issued identification details, and other account data were not affected. Fandango says it is not currently aware of any fraudulent use of customer information as a result of the incident.

## Why it matters

The exposure concerns payment data from the checkout page rather than account credentials. Names, Social Security numbers and government-issued identification details were not affected, and the report describes the potentially exposed set as card and contact data.

Filings with state regulators list 913 affected residents in Oregon and 18 in Vermont. A notice was also submitted to the California Attorney General's office, which requires a copy whenever a breach notification is sent to more than 500 California residents. Fandango has not disclosed the total number of impacted customers, or how the incident occurred; it says the problem has been fixed and its security processes have since been strengthened.

Fandango is a Versant Media Group brand that handles online ticket sales for more than 31,000 movie screens in the US and also runs the Rotten Tomatoes review site.

## What to do

The warning covers certain transactions on the checkout page during that window, not every purchase made in the period. Affected individuals can sign up for two years of free IDX services, including credit monitoring, dark web scanning, identity theft insurance, and managed identity restoration. Fandango says the problem has been fixed and its security processes have since been strengthened, and the report names no customer-side patch or configuration change to apply.
