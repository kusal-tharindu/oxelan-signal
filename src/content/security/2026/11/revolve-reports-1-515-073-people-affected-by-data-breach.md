According to SecurityWeek, Revolve informed the Indiana Attorney General's Office that 1,515,073 individuals were impacted by the data breach it disclosed in October. The company disclosed the incident on October 29 in a filing with the US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC). The report said an unauthorized party broke into some of Revolve's systems, including its e-commerce platform, on September 11. Per the Indiana Attorney General's records, Revolve became aware of the intrusion on September 29.

The report said Revolve's earlier notices to regulators in Iowa, Massachusetts, South Carolina, Texas, and Vermont covered roughly 197,000 people combined. The letter to Iowa counts 3,918 Iowa residents affected. The report said the intruder may have relied on automated means to collect customers' names and contact information, including mailing addresses, along with card numbers and expiration dates. The exposure is limited to shoppers who chose to store a payment method in their online accounts. Revolve's SEC filing does not mention card verification value (CVV) numbers.

This is a data breach at a retailer, not a software flaw, so there is no CVE (Common Vulnerabilities and Exposures), version, or patch. The report said the attack has not been publicly claimed by any hacker group.

The report said Revolve reported the attack to federal law enforcement and enlisted external cybersecurity experts to help with its response. The report said the company has not explained how the hackers got into its systems.
