According to BleepingComputer, a reported cyberattack on Wellington IT exposed sensitive information belonging to members of 50 credit unions, most of them in Ireland. The Belfast-based supplier's services are used by about 50 credit unions, including 43 in Ireland.

## How far the exposure reaches

Minister of State Robert Troy confirmed to RTÉ that Wellington IT was the third-party service provider linked to the incident, which exposed data from about 40 credit unions in Ireland. At least six credit unions have independently disclosed impact, including Capital Credit Union, Phoenix Credit Union, and Limerick & District Credit Union. Wellington IT chief executive Colin Mitchell said the company notified credit union customers of an incident potentially involving information maintained in its CU Online platform.

## What was exposed

The notices list payee records saved in online banking platforms as potentially exposed, including payee name, nickname, IBAN (International Bank Account Number), BIC (Bank Identifier Code), bank account number, sort code, payee address, and payment reference information. Account passwords, personal identification numbers (PINs), payment card details, and online banking credentials were unaffected.

## How the intrusion happened

The attack unfolded between October 2 and 4, 2026, and the attacker accessed private records using valid credentials. Wellington IT informed customers that an automated script made five million requests to exploit an authorization flaw in its infrastructure. The firm said it lacked activity logs that would specify which member data was compromised and cannot verify the impact on individual payees.

## Why it matters

According to the report, Wellington IT notified affected organizations of a data breach posing risks of financial fraud, impersonation, and targeted phishing to their members. Minister Robert Troy called the delay in notifying the credit unions "not acceptable".

## What to do

The advice in the notices is vigilance: members should watch for phishing and impersonation attempts. Wellington IT said it engaged external cybersecurity experts and is collaborating with the Data Protection Commissioner, the National Cyber Security Centre, and the Garda National Cyber Crime Bureau.
