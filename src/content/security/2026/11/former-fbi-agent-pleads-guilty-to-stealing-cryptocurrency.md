Patrick Steven Yaroch, a former agent of the Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) from Ashburn, Virginia, pleaded guilty to wire fraud and money laundering related to stealing cryptocurrency. Yaroch, 37, worked at the FBI Boston Division on an investigative squad focused on an adversarial nation state from 2017 through early 2025. From February 2025 to July 31, 2026, he worked in the FBI's Counterintelligence and Espionage Division, with access to FBI systems that contained cryptocurrency credentials.

## What happened

Beginning no later than December 2024, Yaroch used his position to steal cryptocurrency from wallets controlled by the subjects of various other investigations, by adversarial nation state actors, and by at least one FBI-created wallet. He conducted approximately 100 fraudulent transfers using credentials related to 35 separate FBI investigations and illicitly gained approximately $2,474,380.66. Between November 25, 2025, and January 13, 2026, he transferred approximately $93,082 to one of his own accounts and moved funds between accounts to pay debts.

## Why it matters

The case is about misuse of trusted access rather than a software flaw: the credentials sat in FBI systems Yaroch could reach. His actions compromised FBI sources and methods used in foreign intelligence collection and criminal investigations. According to BleepingComputer, Yaroch self-reported the crypto theft to Department of Justice (DOJ) National Security Division (NSD) and FBI Headquarters employees on July 29, and was arrested at his residence on July 31. BleepingComputer also reported that the FBI fired him on July 31, 2026, after first placing him on administrative leave on July 29.

He also asked ChatGPT how to maximize profit from investments and where to live in the European Union, and after it suggested buying a vineyard in Portugal, he provided power of attorney to lawyers in Portugal on June 15, 2026.

## What to do

This is a criminal matter rather than a product vulnerability: Yaroch pleaded guilty to wire fraud and money laundering. He is scheduled to be sentenced on March 4, 2027, and faces up to 20 years in prison. According to BleepingComputer, he faces up to 20 years in prison for the wire fraud charge and another maximum of 20 years for money laundering.
