According to BleepingComputer, a third party copied payment card data stored on Cleverbridge's platform. Cleverbridge provides e-commerce services to software and SaaS (software as a service) companies.

## What was taken

The report says the stolen information includes payment card numbers, expiration dates, and CVV/CVC security codes, that is, card verification values. Those details were stored on the Cleverbridge platform at the time they were copied.

## How the incident unfolded

The report says Cleverbridge detected unauthorized activity on November 4, 2026, and temporarily took systems offline to contain the breach. Cleverbridge began notifying affected customers on November 20. The company also notified data protection authorities, law enforcement, payment card networks, and payment partners.

## Why it matters

The report states that the incident was not a ransomware attack and that attackers did not encrypt any systems. What it describes instead is copied card data, namely numbers, expiration dates, and security codes, taken from a platform that serves software and SaaS vendors.

## What to do

Cleverbridge is working with TransUnion's Cyberscout to distribute breach notifications and provide affected customers with complimentary credit monitoring and fraud assistance. Customers who receive a notification should follow the enrollment steps it contains to activate that monitoring and assistance. The report says Cleverbridge notified payment card networks and payment partners about the incident.
