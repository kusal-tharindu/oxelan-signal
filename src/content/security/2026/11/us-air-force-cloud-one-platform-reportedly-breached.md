According to BleepingComputer, hackers reportedly breached the US Air Force's Cloud One computing platform. The report says the intrusion was discovered within the past month during an upgrade of the cloud platform. Cloud One, launched in 2017, is an enterprise cloud computing platform managed by the US Air Force for Department of Defense (DoD) agencies.

## What is affected

The report describes Cloud One as the affected platform and says the hackers exploited an unidentified software vulnerability to gain access. The breach disrupted application migrations and software updates. US Cyber Command has reportedly been called in to assess the impact. The attackers, the scope of the attack, and whether any data was stolen are currently unknown, and the Air Force declined to comment on the security or operational status of the affected systems.

## Context

The same report also describes a November 21 reported breach of the National Crime Information Center (NCIC) at the Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) and a reported breach of an open-source intelligence (OSINT) analysis system at the Central Intelligence Agency (CIA). It says it remains unknown whether ARTEX or other artificial intelligence (AI)-powered hacking tools were used in the attacks against the Air Force, FBI, or Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN).

## Why it matters

The report does not establish the attackers, the scope of the attack, or whether any data was stolen, so the operational and disclosure impact remains unresolved.

## What to do

With no fixed version or mitigation step in the cited report, Cloud One users should follow the Air Force's incident guidance and their own incident-response process.
