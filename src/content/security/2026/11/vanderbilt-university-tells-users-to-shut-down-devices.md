Vanderbilt University is responding to an active cybersecurity incident that may impact university devices, systems, and cloud-based services. According to BleepingComputer, the university told students, faculty, and staff to shut down university devices, disconnect from university Wi-Fi, and watch for suspicious activity.

## What is affected

The notice covers university devices, systems, and cloud-based services, and Vanderbilt has not publicly disclosed the nature of the attack or confirmed whether any data was stolen. According to BleepingComputer, the university's instructions ask anyone who notices suspicious activity to shut down the device and contact the IT service desk instead of investigating or troubleshooting the issue themselves.

## Who claimed it

According to BleepingComputer, the BYOD ransomware group claimed responsibility for the attack on its data leak site. The report describes BYOD as a relatively new ransomware gang that launched in 2026 and currently lists 5 other organizations on its data leak site. The same report says the group claimed responsibility last month for an attack on the University of New Mexico Health Sciences Center that caused system outages and disrupted patient care and academic activities.

## Why it matters in production

The incident notice covers devices, systems, and cloud-based services, so the response is not limited to one managed fleet. The first step sits with students, faculty, and staff, who are asked to power devices down rather than work the problem themselves.

## What to do

Shut down university devices, disconnect from university Wi-Fi, and watch for suspicious activity. Do not investigate or troubleshoot suspicious activity yourself. Contact the university's IT service desk instead, and follow its instructions while the incident is active.
