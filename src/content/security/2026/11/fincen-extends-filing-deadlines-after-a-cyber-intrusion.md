According to The Hacker News, FinCEN (Financial Crimes Enforcement Network), the Treasury's financial crimes agency, has disclosed a cyber intrusion and, the same day, issued a relief order, FIN-2026-R002, covering reports it collects from financial institutions. Filing providers reported failures in automated batch submissions beginning November 18, and the bureau has not said whether the intrusion caused those outages.

## What is affected

Covered reports originally due between November 18 and December 29 can now be submitted until December 30. The order applies to financial institutions that used Secure Direct Transfer Mode (SDTM), the channel for sending batches of Bank Secrecy Act (BSA) reports directly from their own servers, as of November 18. It also covers companies required to file a beneficial ownership information report during the covered window, and individuals required to update a FinCEN Identifier during the same window.

## Why it matters

The relief does not suspend other BSA obligations: the duty to monitor for suspicious transactions stays in force. Urgent suspicious activity information can still reach law enforcement through the Financial Institutions Hotline, 1-866-556-3974.

The agency has not said when the access began, whether any data was viewed or taken, or who was responsible, and no group has publicly claimed the intrusion. In December 2024, a China-linked group used a compromised BeyondTrust API key in an earlier Treasury breach; no link between the two incidents has been reported.

## What to do

Filers should confirm whether their institution used SDTM as of November 18, which determines whether the order covers their batch submissions. Covered reports can be submitted until December 30 instead of the original due dates. Suspicious-activity monitoring has to continue, and urgent information should go to the Financial Institutions Hotline at 1-866-556-3974.
