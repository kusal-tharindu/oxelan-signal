According to SecurityWeek, JPMorgan Chase, Visa and Mastercard disclosed incidents involving outside service providers after the N0n extortion group claimed it stole their customers' data. JPMorgan said some companies providing services to the bank had been hit by a cyberattack. The bank believes attackers accessed certain marketing data held by vendors, which may include some customer names and email addresses.

## What is affected

Visa said the claims are associated with an incident involving a third-party service provider in Asia Pacific. Visa said neither its own systems nor payment data such as card numbers had been impacted. Mastercard said an incident at a third-party provider exposed a limited amount of email addresses from a small subset of cardholders enrolled in a specific benefit program.

Mastercard said its payment network was not affected and cardholders can still use their cards. None of the companies has identified the affected providers or said how many customers were impacted.

## Why it matters

The disclosures show how an incident at a marketing or benefit vendor can reach customer contact data even when the payment network itself is not affected. On November 16, N0n claimed to have obtained JPMorgan-related engagement data. Reuters' review of the JPMorgan-related data found over 16,000 customer emails.

The review also found what appear to be 124 email addresses belonging to JPMorgan bank employees. N0n emerged in September and currently lists roughly 200 alleged victims on its leak site.

## What to do

JPMorgan customers should watch for direct notice from the bank, which plans, where appropriate, to reach out to impacted customers and give them free credit monitoring.
