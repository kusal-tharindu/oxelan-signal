According to SecurityWeek, the European Commission confirmed it was hit by an AI-assisted cyberattack that disrupted several of its services. The report said the attack was detected on October 22 and confirmed on October 26.

The report said the Commission temporarily took down several applications as a protective and preventive measure, and that most of them have since been restored. It also said the investigation has found no evidence of a data breach affecting Commission staff.

Commission spokesperson Thomas Regnier did not attribute the incident to a known group and said he was not aware of a ransom demand related to the attack, according to the report. Regnier confirmed that hackers used an AI tool during the attack but did not name it.

## What is affected

The report, citing Politico, said the impacted services included the HR platform Sysper, the medical reimbursement system MyPMO, and translation systems. Politico also reported that the hackers potentially compromised large amounts of data and caused disruptions lasting over 10 days.

## The wider AI-attack context

CERT-EU, described in the report as the cybersecurity arm of the European Union, warned of threat actors using the AI-powered penetration-testing framework Artex to target its constituency. CERT-EU assessed that public availability of Artex will likely accelerate adoption by threat actors, resulting in more frequent, faster, and more scalable automated attacks with a higher success rate. The report said Artex has been used to hack Barracuda, Railway, ConnectWise customers, and other organizations.

## What to do

The only response described in the cited reporting is the one the Commission took: it temporarily took down several applications as a protective and preventive measure, and most of them have since been restored. The cited sources name no fixed version or workaround for other organizations to apply.
