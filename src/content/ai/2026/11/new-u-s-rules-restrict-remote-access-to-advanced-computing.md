## What changed

According to SecurityWeek, the Trump administration issued new rules to restrict remote access to advanced U.S. computing chips and servers. The report says the rule took effect immediately and was issued by the Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) under the Commerce Department. Under the rule, overseas data centers that serve companies in countries including China, Russia, Iran and Cuba are required to identify their end users. It also adds case-by-case licensing requirements for chip sales to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil.

## Why it matters

The rule says Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil present a higher-than-normal risk of diversion to unauthorized destinations. The U.S. bans sales of its most advanced artificial intelligence (AI) chips to China. According to the report, the rule was issued days after Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia CEO. The report also says Singapore-based Megaspeed International is suspected of diverting advanced Nvidia chips to China in violation of U.S. export controls.

## What to do

Operators of overseas data centers that serve companies in China, Russia, Iran or Cuba should review the end-user identification requirement. Chip sellers should check whether sales to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand or Brazil now require case-by-case licenses. The report notes that U.S. Representative Michael Lawler introduced a bill to broaden U.S. export controls to include remote access, and that the bill has passed the House.
