## What changed

OpenAI is expanding its ChatGPT for Academic Researchers program to give up to 200,000 researchers worldwide free access to ChatGPT. The program began as a pilot in July. Applications for the next round open Monday, November 23.

OpenAI will award 50,000 new grants on a first-come, first-served basis. Each grant gives the recipient and up to two collaborators free access to ChatGPT Plus. By default, OpenAI does not use data shared through the program to train its models.

## Why it matters

Since the pilot began in July, 20,000 researchers in 33 countries have joined the program. OpenAI is publishing new research titled Science at the Frontier: How Scientists Use ChatGPT, examining how more than 1,800 researchers used ChatGPT during the summer pilot. Surveyed participants estimated that, without AI (artificial intelligence), the same research work at the same quality would have taken nearly twice as long.

## What the program offers

Based on researcher feedback, OpenAI is moving away from lottery-based selection and will provide access to the first 50,000 applications that meet eligibility requirements. The pilot gave researchers access to ChatGPT Pro. OpenAI is offering ChatGPT Plus for new grants because most pilot participants would have stayed within Plus usage limits. Each grant for this round covers the recipient and up to two collaborators, supporting up to 150,000 researchers. OpenAI's goal for the program as a whole is to reach 200,000 researchers, double the goal announced when the pilot launched in July.

OpenAI also contributed $5 million to a new Simons Foundation initiative to help mathematicians examine, understand, and build on results produced by AI. The Rosalind Academic Plan offers up to 30 seats at no cost for up to 12 months to eligible labs at U.S. universities. Access to GPT-Rosalind requires approval through OpenAI's trusted-access process.

## What to do

Eligible researchers should prepare to apply when the next round opens on November 23; OpenAI will provide access to the first 50,000 applications that meet the eligibility requirements. Researchers seeking GPT-Rosalind access must go through OpenAI's trusted-access approval process. Teams should also note that OpenAI does not use program data to train its models by default.
