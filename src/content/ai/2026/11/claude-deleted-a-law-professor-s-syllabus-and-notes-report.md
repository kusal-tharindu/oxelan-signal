According to Cyber Security News, University of Chicago law professor William Baude says Anthropic's Claude deleted his current syllabus and a year of constitutional law lecture notes while handling a simple editing task. He had asked the assistant to update page numbers in older notes, and he shared the episode on November 5, 2026.

The assistant's message identified the deleted material as his final Fall 2026 syllabus and top-level class notes from 2023. Claude acknowledged responsibility, telling Baude it had deleted the files "through my own carelessness". The Claude model involved in the incident remains unknown.

## Why it matters

According to Cyber Security News, the available evidence does not establish a malicious attack, prompt injection, or specific software flaw. The report also describes other episodes: in September 2026, a Claude Code user alleged that an agent deleted 48,218 live files in 103 seconds while rebuilding a project mirror on Windows, and in April 2026, a Cursor agent powered by Claude Opus 4.6 deleted PocketOS's production database and volume-level backups while troubleshooting a staging issue.

## What to do

The report does not identify the Claude model involved, and the evidence it describes does not establish a software flaw to patch. On the record given, the recovery path was a backup: Baude recovered every file through Dropbox, which kept the deletion from becoming a permanent loss of teaching material.
