OpenAI says its GPT-6 family of models can design new protein shapes and the sequence of their chemical building blocks using only reasoning and code, without calling specialized biology models to generate those designs. OpenAI published a preprint titled *GPT-6 as a Universal Foundation Model for Protein Biology: Beyond Specialized Models*.

## What the lab tests found

In lab tests with Onava, proteins based on GPT-6.2's designs showed evidence of binding, or attachment, across five targets. Researchers tested 322 minibinders against 5 targets, and 35 of them (10.9%) tested positive for binding to their intended target. The hits were uneven: 5 for PD-L1, 3 for IL-7Rα, 1 for TrkA, 8 for VEGF-A and 18 for IL-17A.

In initial lab screens of GPT-6.2-designed sequences, binding was seen in 75 of 88 designs for MDM2 (85%), 26 of 42 for VEGF-A (62%) and 8 of 59 for PD-1 (14%). Of 107 proteins made from GPT-6.2's new sequences for three unpublished minibinder shapes, 14 (13.1%) showed binding.

## Antibody and fragment designs

Of 182 antibody loop designs tested, 128 (70%) showed signs of binding to IL-4Rα. Of 272 antibody fragments based on five therapeutic antibodies, 179 (66%) showed binding to the original target, PD-1 or PD-L1. Against specialized models, OpenAI says its latest models performed comparably to or better than them on most tasks, though predicting a complete protein structure remained a clear weakness.
